# Show and tell — talking points

Published page (video + these notes): https://claude.ai/code/artifact/5f6aa843-a55e-4d85-81a5-38c75abbab4e
The tool itself: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0fc98b73-7b76-4899-a638-97cedc2ca320
Video file: `docs/media/local-law-review-demo.mp4` (62s, silent, captioned)

## What it is
- A register of all 121 operative clauses, read out of the law itself, each taking a decision, an owner and a note.
- A backward-planned program: nine phases, twelve gates, with the sunset and the caretaker period drawn as immovable.
- The change backlog in one place, including items sitting since 2019, scored for impact and effort.
- The engagement plan with an IAP2 level against every activity.
- A pilot register — seven pilots under existing powers, each with a stated measure, all reporting before drafting locks.
- Risks and decisions, scored and owned.

## How it makes our lives easier
- Turns "review the local law" into something countable: 121 clauses, 26 without a decision.
- Kills the version problem — one page, not eleven copies of a spreadsheet.
- Answers "are we on track and what's blocking us" in ten seconds.
- Forces held-over items out at the start, with a date and a name on each.
- Keeps pilots honest: hypothesis and measure written down before they start.
- Protects the margin — four weeks between commencement and sunset, displayed permanently.

## Transparency and collaboration
- Every unit works from the same register.
- Every change is stamped with who and when, so decisions have an author.
- Nothing parks silently; the raised date is visible on every backlog item.
- Uncertainty is labelled on the face of the tool ("Confirm with Governance"), not buried.
- The same page runs the standup, the Executive update and the Councillor briefing.

## The AI angle
- Built in a day from the PDF of the existing law; the clause register was read out of the instrument, not typed.
- The shift is not that AI wrote code — it is that a purpose-built tool now beats bending the work to a generic template.
- Cheap to change means it is allowed to be wrong; the process can move without a two-year lock-in.
- Content stays in plain files in our own repository, independent of this tool.
- The pattern generalises to any long statutory process with a fixed deadline.

## Where to be careful
- Starting content is drafted, not decided — only the clause structure and sunset date come from the law.
- Statutory steps need Governance and Legal confirmation before the first Council report.
- Talk to Information Management before real content goes in.
- Not a submissions system; scope that separately at G6.

## The cost question
A day, and no procurement. The honest follow-up: the value is in whether the PM runs the
review in it. A tool nobody opens is worse than a spreadsheet everybody does.
