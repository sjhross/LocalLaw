# Brief for the project manager

## The argument in one paragraph

The 2018 local law ends on 19 July 2028 and cannot be extended. Between now and
then Council has to run an internal review, consult a community that will care
intensely about two or three of the questions, pilot the changes that carry
operational risk, draft a new instrument, exhibit it, hear submitters, resolve
to make it, gazette it and commence it — and be finished before the October 2028
election caretaker period removes the ability to decide anything. That is not a
long time. The tool exists to make the backward plan visible and to stop the
held-over backlog surfacing after the scope is locked.

## The five things it is built to hold

**1. One date, held in front of everyone.** The sunset is on every screen. So is
the gap between the planned commencement and the sunset — currently four weeks.
That number is the whole safety margin and it should never be spent quietly.

**2. A clause register nobody can hide in.** All 121 operative clauses, each
needing a disposition and an owner. Gate G3 is simply "no clause left at Not
started". It converts a vague instruction to review the law into a countable
piece of work that can be reported on weekly.

**3. The backlog, in the open.** Items have been sitting with managers since
2019. A review is when they surface, usually late and usually all at once. The
proposals module forces each one to a disposition — in scope, out of scope, or
deferred with a stated reason — before the scope locks at G3, and records who
raised it and when.

**4. Pilots that can actually change the drafting.** Every pilot listed runs
under powers Council already has: permit conditions, operator agreements,
policy. None waits on the new law. All report by G4 in June 2027, because
drafting instructions are approved in September 2027 and evidence arriving after
that is evidence for the next review.

**5. Consultation before the positions harden.** The statutory exhibition is a
poor place to discover an objection. The issues-stage work in phase 3 is what
makes the formal stage survivable, and each activity carries an IAP2 level so
the promise made to participants is explicit.

## What to do with it in the first week

1. Overwrite the gate dates with the real Council meeting calendar, working back
   from each agenda close date. The Q1 gates are the tight ones — there is no
   January meeting.
2. Put the real names against the gates and the clause owners.
3. Run the held-over surgery (E02) and empty the Parked column.
4. Send the flagged gates to Governance and Legal for the statutory
   confirmation, and re-cut the dates if the notice period differs from the
   assumption.
5. Decide D05: whether the Footpath Trading and Keeping of Animals policies stay
   incorporated under cl. 19 or move into the law itself. It shapes the drafting
   scope and it is still open.

## What it deliberately does not do

It is not a submission-management system, a permit system, or a drafting tool.
When the exhibition opens in late 2027 the submission volume — particularly on
cat containment — will need something purpose-built or a properly resourced
manual process. That should be scoped at G6, not improvised at G8.
